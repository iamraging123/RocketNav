01

The filter

eskf.cpp · 739 lines, no dependencies
Error stateDimUnitsBounded by
δp3mGNSS position · baro (vertical)
δv3m/sGNSS velocity · baro damping
δθ3radgravity when still · mag heading
δbg3rad/saveraged in the alignment window
δba3m/s²observable once anchored
δbb1mGNSS-vs-baro altitude, weather drift

The attitude error lives in the world frame, not the body. That one choice makes heading error exactly the down component of δθ — so the magnetometer structurally cannot corrupt roll or pitch — and it makes the transition matrix exact: F is nilpotent, and Φ = I + Fdt + F²dt²/2 + F³dt³/6 is the full exponential, not a truncation.

Every update is Joseph-form with a χ² gate at 99%, and the baro noise model inflates with speed² and through the transonic — driven by the estimated state, never by flight-phase logic.

Altitude never waits for GNSS. The baro-damped vertical channel runs from the end of alignment, indoors or out; a late fix upgrades to full navigation with the altitude kept continuous.

02

Flight sequence

FiltState — the enum, verbatim
FS_0

Init

Power on. The first IMU sample arms the filter and starts the clock.

FS_1

Align

2.5 s of measured stillness. Gravity gives tilt, the mag gives heading, and the averaged gyro rate is the calibration.

FS_2

Wait fix

Attitude and the baro vertical channel run. The first valid GNSS fix anchors the world origin.

FS_3

Run

Full 3-D navigation: position, velocity, attitude, and three bias estimates, output at 50 Hz.

FS_4

Att-only

No fix in 20 s? Fly on attitude + baro altitude. Nothing blocks on the sky.

The branch heals itself: a fix arriving in FS_4 anchors late and upgrades 4 → 3, rebuilding position and re-seeding the baro bias so altitude stays continuous — a late anchor is identical to an on-time one.
